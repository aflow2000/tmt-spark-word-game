The AI Infrastructure Race

Compute is scaling faster than the grid, the supply chain and the construction market can follow. This month: where the bottlenecks really are.

AI Data Centers Are Running Into the Power Wall

Racks that drew 5–10 kW a few years ago are being specified at 100 kW and beyond, and campuses once measured in megawatts are now planned in gigawatts. The constraint on AI growth is shifting from chips and capital to electricity and cooling.

Why it matters

Site selection is becoming an energy question first and a real estate question second. Power availability — not land price — now sets where AI capacity gets built, how fast it delivers and what it costs.

Data CentersInfrastructure 3 min read

The 800V Data Center

To feed ever-denser racks, operators are rethinking the electrical backbone of the building — moving from traditional lower-voltage AC distribution toward higher-voltage DC. Fewer conversions mean less wasted energy, less copper and less heat.

Why it matters

A different electrical architecture changes switchgear, busways, commissioning and the skills contractors need on site. New builds and retrofits will diverge — and the supply chain will follow the new spec.

Industry impacted
Data centersElectrical contractorsEquipment makers
EnergyCapital Markets 4 min read

Nuclear Meets AI

Hyperscalers are signing long-term power agreements with existing nuclear plants and backing small modular reactors. AI demand wants firm, carbon-free power at gigawatt scale — and interconnection queues are too slow for AI timelines.

Why it matters

Energy strategy is becoming a core part of digital infrastructure planning. Decade-long power bets will shape where campuses are sited, who partners with whom, and which markets win the next wave of investment.

Industry impacted
EnergyData centersInvestorsRegulators
SemiconductorsConstruction 5 min read

The Semiconductor Construction Boom

Multibillion-dollar fab projects are reshaping construction demand across the United States. A leading-edge fab is among the most complex buildings ever delivered — cleanrooms, vibration control, ultra-pure water and thousands of skilled trades on one site.

Why it matters

Fab mega-projects absorb skilled labor, equipment and materials for years, tightening cost and schedule for everyone building near them. Suppliers and housing follow the fabs — and so does regional real estate demand.

Industry impacted
SemiconductorsConstructionRegional markets
AIRobotics 3 min read

Robotics Leave the Lab

Foundation models are reaching into the physical world. Humanoid and mobile robots are moving from demo videos to paid pilots in manufacturing and logistics — the first commercial test of "physical AI" at scale.

Why it matters

If robots work, industrial space changes: more power, different layouts, new safety zones and different labor profiles. Early adopters will quietly re-spec warehouses and plants over the next lease cycle.

The Interconnection Queue Is the New Zoning Board

In major markets, connecting a large project to the grid can take longer than designing and building it. Grid access — not permits — increasingly decides what gets built where.

Why it matters

Developers are buying power positions the way they once bought land positions. Expect premiums for energized sites and creative behind-the-meter deals.

Capital Markets4 min

Who Pays for the AI Buildout?

Hundreds of billions in data center capex is being financed by a new mix of infrastructure funds, private credit, joint ventures and hyperscaler balance sheets.

Why it matters

How the buildout is financed shapes who owns digital infrastructure in ten years — and how resilient the market is if AI demand growth wobbles.

Construction3 min

The Two-Year Transformer

Lead times for large transformers, switchgear and generators still stretch past two years. Procurement now drives the construction schedule, not the other way around.

Why it matters

Teams that lock equipment early win. Expect more owner-furnished equipment, more standardization and more appetite for modular electrical rooms.

Telecom3 min

Fiber's Quiet Land Grab

Long-haul and metro fiber routes are being built and bought along the corridors connecting AI data center clusters. Connectivity is following compute.

Why it matters

Fiber access is becoming a site-selection criterion alongside power. Carriers, developers and investors are converging on the same maps.

Media4 min

Streaming Rebundles Itself

Consolidation, ad-supported tiers and record sports-rights deals are pushing streaming back toward something that looks a lot like the bundle it replaced.

Why it matters

Media economics drive studio space, production hubs and the tech stacks behind delivery — all of it real estate and infrastructure in disguise.

TelecomWorkplace3 min

Private 5G Gets to Work

Ports, factories and large campuses are deploying their own cellular networks where Wi-Fi falls short — reliable wireless for machines, not just people.

Why it matters

Automation-heavy occupiers will start asking landlords about spectrum the way they ask about power. Connectivity is becoming a building amenity.

The Office Is a Product Again

Fast-growing AI companies are competing for talent with dense, amenity-rich, high-energy offices — and they need them delivered in months, not years.

Why it matters

Speed of fit-out is becoming a competitive advantage. Landlords and project teams that can deliver fast, flexible space will win the AI expansion wave.

Sensor networks and AI-driven building operations are finally clearing the ROI bar — cutting energy use and operating cost in buildings that install them well.

Why it matters

Efficiency requirements and energy prices are turning smart-building tech from a nice-to-have into an underwriting line item.

About TMT Spark

Educate first. Build relationships second. Business follows naturally.

TMT Spark is how Turner & Townsend's Technology, Media & Telecommunications practice shows up in the industry — as a publisher, a convener and a community rather than a cold caller. Instead of approaching prospective clients with a sales message, Spark invites industry leaders into conversations about where the industry is going. The public experience is media and education. What follows is relationships.

"The conversation comes before the pitch."
Internal view — how Spark supports Turner & Townsend's TMT strategy

The TMT Spark network effect

Two ways to meet an executive.

The platform doesn't replace business development — it replaces the cold open. Compare the paths.

Traditional outreach

1

Cold email. "Can Turner & Townsend help you manage your next construction project?"

2

Sales meeting request. A calendar invite from someone they've never met.

3

Low response rate. The message competes with every other vendor in the inbox.

Transactional from the first word — and easy to ignore.

The TMT Spark approach

1

Industry research. Spark's editorial work surfaces who is building, expanding and investing.

2

Invite them onto TMT Spark. A panel, a roundtable, an interview — a reason to talk that isn't a pitch.

3

Discuss their expertise. The conversation is about their world: their constraints, their plans, their view of the market.

4

Build the relationship. Shared interests, real content, follow-ups that feel natural.

5

Understand their priorities. Growth plans, cost pressures, infrastructure constraints — heard firsthand.

6

Introduce Turner & Townsend capabilities when appropriate. Only where Turner & Townsend can actually help — and only when the timing is right.

Reciprocal from the first word — and worth responding to.
" A technology executive may not respond to

"Can we sell you cost management services?"

A pitch with nothing in it for them. Deleted in seconds.

" But they may respond to

"We're interviewing leaders about how AI companies are managing the infrastructure constraints created by rapid growth. We'd love your perspective."

An invitation to be the expert. That conversation creates a genuine relationship.
